Euclides de Mègara (c. 435-365 aC), deixeble de Sòcrates, va fundar l'escola megàrica. Va unir la recerca socràtica del bé amb la doctrina de Parmènides: el bé és un de sol, encara que rebi molts noms (saviesa, déu, intel·ligència), i el contrari del bé no existeix. Els seus seguidors es van fer famosos pels seus arguments i paradoxes lògiques. Després de la mort de Sòcrates, el 399, Plató i altres deixebles es van refugiar a casa seva.
Euclides of Megara (c. 435-365 BC), a disciple of Socrates, founded the Megarian school. He joined the Socratic search for the good with the doctrine of Parmenides: the good is one, even though it goes by many names (wisdom, god, intelligence), and the opposite of the good does not exist. His followers became famous for their arguments and logical paradoxes. After Socrates' death in 399, Plato and other disciples took refuge in his house.

Delfos era l'oracle més famós de Grècia. Ciutats i particulars consultaven Apol·lo a través de la Pítia, una dona de Delfos que parlava en trànsit des de l'àdyton del temple; els sacerdots posaven les seves respostes per escrit, de vegades en vers. Respostes cèlebres: a Cresos (si atacava els perses, destruiria un gran imperi: el seu) i a Atenes el 480 (confiar en els «murs de fusta», les naus).
Delphi was the most famous oracle in Greece. Cities and individuals consulted Apollo through the Pythia, a woman of Delphi who spoke in a trance from the temple's adyton; the priests wrote down her answers, sometimes in verse. It was administered by the Amphictyony, a league of neighbouring peoples. Famous answers: to Croesus (if he attacked the Persians he would destroy a great empire: his own) and to Athens in 480 (trust in the "wooden walls", the ships).

Policlet d'Argos (actiu cap al 460-410 aC) va ser un dels grans escultors grecs. Al seu tractat, el Cànon, va fixar les proporcions ideals del cos humà a partir de les relacions entre les seves parts, i el va il·lustrar amb una estàtua de bronze, el Dorífor (portador de llança). Les seves figures mostren el contrapposto: el pes sobre una cama i l'altra relaxada. Les coneixem sobretot per còpies romanes en marbre. Argos, democràtica i enemiga d'Esparta, va tenir una gran escola de bronzistes.
Polyclitus of Argos (active around 460-410 BC) was one of the great Greek sculptors. In his treatise, the Canon, he set out the ideal proportions of the human body based on the relations between its parts, and illustrated it with a bronze statue, the Doryphoros (spear-bearer). His figures show contrapposto: the weight on one leg and the other relaxed. We know them mainly from Roman marble copies. Argos, democratic and hostile to Sparta, had a great school of bronze-casters.

A large statue is cast in pieces: head, arms, torso. Then they're joined, polished, and the eyes of stone and glass are added, the lips and nipples in copper, the eyelashes in bronze. A well-made statue seems to breathe.
