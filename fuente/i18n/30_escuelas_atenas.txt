Being able to sing to the lyre at a banquet was a sign of good breeding.

No tota la música és igual. Cada mode (harmonia) és una manera d'ordenar els sons de l'escala, i cadascun té el seu caràcter: el dori és greu i viril, el frigi, entusiasta, propi dels cultes d'èxtasi; el lidi i el mixolidi, tous i planyívols.
Not all music is the same. Each mode (harmonia) is a way of ordering the notes of the scale, and each has its own character: the Dorian is grave and manly, the Phrygian enthusiastic, fit for ecstatic cults; the Lydian and Mixolydian soft and plaintive.

At banquets, each guest sings a skolion, a short song, holding the myrtle branch.

The paidotribes taught wrestling and the pentathlon events (running, jumping, discus, javelin and wrestling) in the palaestra. Athletes trained naked, oiled and dusted, and afterwards scraped it off with a strigil. Sport prepared men for war and for the great Panhellenic contests, like the Olympic Games, and was part of the ideal of beauty and virtue (kalokagathia).

A la lluita (pále) es guanya tombant el rival tres vegades: una caiguda compta quan toca a terra amb l'esquena, les espatlles o el maluc. Es lluita dret, agafant coll, braços i cintura; estan prohibits els cops i mossegar. El pancraci, en canvi, barreja lluita i cops de puny, i gairebé tot s'hi val.
In wrestling (pale) you win by throwing your opponent three times: a fall counts when his back, shoulders or hip touch the ground. You wrestle standing, gripping neck, arms and waist; punches and biting are forbidden. The pankration, by contrast, mixes wrestling and punching, and almost anything goes.

El pentatló reuneix cinc proves: la cursa de l'estadi (a Olímpia, uns 192 metres), el salt de llargada, el disc, la javelina i la lluita. El guanya l'atleta més complet, no el més fort: Aristòtil dirà que els pentatletes són els més bells.

En arribar a la palestra, el noi es despulla a l'apodyterion, s'unta d'oli que porta en un flascó rodó, l'aríbal, i es tira pols fina. Després de l'exercici es rasca la barreja d'oli, pols i suor amb l'estrígil, un rascador corbat de bronze, i es renta.
On reaching the palaestra, the boy undresses in the apodyterion, oils himself from a small round flask, the aryballos, and sprinkles on fine dust. After exercise he scrapes off the mixture of oil, dust and sweat with the strigil, a curved bronze scraper, and washes.

The paidotribes keeps watch with a forked rod in his hand: with it he corrects postures and punishes fouls. The boys train to the sound of the aulos, and in the palaestra people also talk: Socrates came here to speak with the young men, as in Plato's Charmides and Lysis.
