Cartago, fundada per colons fenicis de Tir cap al 814 aC segons la tradició, va dominar la Mediterrània occidental i va lluitar contra Roma en tres guerres púniques (264-241, 218-201 i 149-146 aC). En la tercera, Escipió Emilià la va prendre i la va arrasar, i el seu territori es va convertir en la província d'Àfrica. Un segle després, Cèsar i August van fundar sobre les ruïnes la colònia romana de Cartago, que va arribar a ser una de les ciutats més grans de l'Imperi i el port del blat africà per a Roma. La llengua i alguns cultes púnics van sobreviure segles.
Carthage, founded by Phoenician settlers from Tyre around 814 BC according to tradition, dominated the western Mediterranean and fought Rome in three Punic Wars (264-241, 218-201 and 149-146 BC). In the third, Scipio Aemilianus took it and razed it, and its territory became the province of Africa. A century later Caesar and Augustus founded the Roman colony of Carthage on the ruins; it became one of the largest cities of the Empire and the port for African grain bound for Rome. The Punic language and some Punic cults survived for centuries.

The Byrsa and the Capitol

@35bed6da
La Birsa va ser la ciutadella de la Cartago púnica. Segons la llegenda, la reina Dido, fugida de Tir, va demanar al rei del lloc tanta terra com pogués abastar una pell de bou; la va tallar en tires finíssimes i va envoltar amb elles tot el turó. Els romans van aplanar el cim, enterrant sota el reompliment les cases púniques, i hi van aixecar a sobre el fòrum, la basílica i el Capitoli de la colònia.
The Byrsa was the citadel of Punic Carthage. According to legend, Queen Dido, fleeing from Tyre, asked the local king for as much land as an ox hide could cover; she cut it into very thin strips and surrounded the whole hill with them. The Romans levelled the summit, burying the Punic houses beneath the fill, and built the colony's forum, basilica and Capitol on top.

Carthage had two connected harbours: a rectangular one for merchant ships and, behind it, a circular one for the war fleet, with an island in the middle where the admiral lived and slipways for some two hundred and twenty ships around it. Today they are used by the grain ships: according to Flavius Josephus, Africa feeds Rome eight months of the year.

Thermopylae means "the hot gates": springs of hot water smelling of sulphur rise here. The locals call them the pots, and beside them stands an altar of Heracles. They say Athena, or Hephaestus, made these waters spring up so that Heracles could bathe after one of his labours. The water leaves white crusts on the ground.
