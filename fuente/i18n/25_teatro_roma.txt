Publius Ovidius Naso (43 BC-AD 17/18) was the most brilliant poet of Augustus' age: he wrote love poems (Amores, The Art of Love), the Heroides, the Fasti on the Roman calendar and the Metamorphoses, some two hundred and fifty myths of transformation linked together in fifteen books. Titus Livius (59 BC-AD 17), born in Patavium (Padua), wrote in 142 books the history of Rome from its founding (Ab urbe condita) down to 9 BC. Thirty-five survive. In his preface he says history is useful for seeing examples of what to imitate and what to avoid. Augustus treated him as a friend, though he called him a «Pompeian» for his sympathy towards the Republic. Thanks to him we know most of the legends of early Rome.

La comèdia romana adaptava comèdies gregues, sobretot de Menandre, amb personatges grecs amb mantell (palliata). Plaute (c. 254-184 aC) va escriure moltes comèdies, de les quals se'n conserven 21, plenes de cançons, jocs de paraules i esclaus astuts. Terenci (c. 190-159 aC), un antic esclau de Cartago, va escriure sis comèdies més refinades i realistes. En època imperial el públic preferia el mim i la pantomima, però les comèdies antigues es continuaven llegint a les escoles i de vegades es reposaven.
Roman comedy adapted Greek comedies, especially Menander's, with Greek characters in cloaks (palliata). Plautus (c. 254-184 BC) wrote many comedies, of which 21 survive, full of songs, wordplay and cunning slaves. Terence (c. 190-159 BC), a former slave from Carthage, wrote six more refined and realistic comedies. In imperial times audiences preferred mime and pantomime, but the old comedies were still read in schools and sometimes revived.

Els actors som tots homes, també els que fan de dones, i gairebé tots esclaus o llibertos. Portem màscara, perruca i el mantell grec. Un tocador de tíbia, la flauta doble, acompanya les parts cantades. L'escenari representa un carrer amb dues o tres cases, i tot passa davant de les portes. Abans calia muntar teatres de fusta; des de Pompeu tenim teatres de pedra. I al final, sempre el mateix: «Aplaudiu!».
We actors are all men, even those who play women, and nearly all slaves or freedmen. We wear masks, wigs and the Greek cloak. A tibia player, on the double pipe, accompanies the sung parts. The stage shows a street with two or three houses, and everything happens in front of the doors. Theatres used to be built of wood each time; since Pompey we've had stone theatres. And at the end, always the same: «Applaud!».

Theatre reached Rome from Etruria and from the Greek cities of southern Italy. In 240 BC Livius Andronicus, a Greek from Tarentum, staged the first tragedy and the first comedy in Latin.
