@c10727f2
Al principi va ser el Caos, el buit. Després van néixer Gea, la Terra, i Eros. Gea va engendrar Urà, el Cel, i amb ell els Titans, els Cíclops i els Hecatonquirs. Urà tancava els seus fills al si de la Terra, fins que Gea va donar una falç a Cronos, el més petit dels Titans, que va castrar el seu pare; de l'escuma del mar va néixer Afrodita. Cronos, advertit que un fill el destronaria, devorava els que li donava Rea. Ella li va lliurar una pedra embolicada amb bolquers i va amagar Zeus a Creta. Ja adult, Zeus va obligar Cronos a vomitar els seus germans, va vèncer els Titans en una guerra de deu anys i els va tancar al Tàrtar. Després va derrotar el monstre Tifó i va repartir els honors entre els déus.
In the beginning was Chaos, the void. Then Gaia, the Earth, and Eros were born. Gaia brought forth Uranus, the Sky, and with him the Titans, the Cyclopes and the Hundred-Handers. Uranus shut his children up inside the Earth, until Gaia gave a sickle to Cronus, the youngest Titan, who castrated his father; from the sea foam Aphrodite was born. Cronus, warned that a son would overthrow him, swallowed the children Rhea bore him. She gave him a stone wrapped in swaddling clothes and hid Zeus in Crete. Grown up, Zeus forced Cronus to vomit up his brothers and sisters, defeated the Titans in a ten-year war and shut them in Tartarus. Then he defeated the monster Typhon and shared out honours among the gods.

El tità Prometeu va enganyar Zeus a Mecone: va repartir un bou sacrificat i li va fer triar entre els ossos coberts de greix lluent i la carn amagada a l'estómac. Zeus va triar el greix; per això els homes cremen els ossos per als déus i mengen la carn. Zeus, irritat, els va prendre el foc, i Prometeu el va robar amagat en una canya buida. Als homes els va enviar Pandora, la primera dona, adornada per tots els déus. Pandora va obrir una gran gerra i en van sortir tots els mals; a dins només hi va quedar l'esperança.
The Titan Prometheus tricked Zeus at Mecone: he divided a sacrificed ox and let him choose between the bones covered in gleaming fat and the meat hidden in the stomach. Zeus chose the fat; that's why men burn the bones for the gods and eat the meat. Zeus, angered, took fire away from them, and Prometheus stole it hidden in a hollow reed. As punishment, Zeus chained him to a rock, where an eagle devoured his liver every day, until Heracles freed him. To men he sent Pandora, the first woman, adorned by all the gods. Pandora opened a great jar and all the evils came out; only hope remained inside.

Segons Hesíode, els déus van crear successives races d'homes: la d'or, que vivia sense treball ni vellesa; la de plata, neci i impia; la de bronze, violenta, que es va destruir a si mateixa; la dels herois, que van lluitar a Tebes i a Troia; i la de ferro, la nostra, condemnada al treball i al dolor. Un altre mite explica que Zeus va enviar un diluvi per acabar amb els homes. Deucalió, fill de Prometeu, i la seva esposa Pirra es van salvar en una arca i, aconsellats pels déus, van llançar pedres per sobre de les espatlles: de les d'ell van néixer homes; de les d'ella, dones.
According to Hesiod, the gods created successive races of men: the golden race, which lived without toil or old age; the silver, foolish and impious; the bronze, violent, which destroyed itself; the race of heroes, who fought at Thebes and Troy; and the iron race, ours, condemned to toil and pain. Another myth tells that Zeus sent a flood to wipe out mankind. Deucalion, son of Prometheus, and his wife Pyrrha survived in an ark and, on the gods' advice, threw stones over their shoulders: from his stones men were born; from hers, women.

Acrisi, rei d'Argos, va saber per un oracle que el mataria el seu nét, i va tancar la seva filla Dànae en una cambra de bronze. Zeus hi va entrar convertit en pluja d'or i va néixer Perseu. Acrisi va llançar mare i fill al mar en un cofre, que va arribar a l'illa de Sèrifos. Allà el rei Polidectes, que desitjava Dànae, va enviar Perseu a portar el cap de la Gorgona Medusa, la mirada de la qual convertia en pedra. Amb l'ajuda d'Atena i d'Hermes, unes sandàlies alades, el barret que fa invisible i un sarró, Perseu la va decapitar apartant la vista; del seu coll van néixer el cavall Pegàs i Crisaor. En tornar va salvar Andròmeda d'un monstre marí. Anys després, en uns jocs, el seu disc va colpejar per accident un vell: era Acrisi.
Acrisius, king of Argos, learned from an oracle that his grandson would kill him, and shut his daughter Danae in a bronze chamber. Zeus came in as a shower of gold and Perseus was born. Acrisius cast mother and son into the sea in a chest, which reached the island of Seriphos. There King Polydectes, who desired Danae, sent Perseus to fetch the head of the Gorgon Medusa, whose gaze turned people to stone. With the help of Athena and Hermes, winged sandals, the cap of invisibility and a pouch, Perseus beheaded her while looking away; from her neck sprang the horse Pegasus and Chrysaor. On his way back he saved Andromeda from a sea monster. Years later, at some games, his discus accidentally struck an old man: it was Acrisius.

Fill de Zeus i de la mortal Alcmena, Hèracles va ser perseguit des del bressol per Hera, que li va enviar dues serps que el nen va escanyar. Ja adult, Hera el va fer embogir i va matar els seus propis fills. Per purificar-se va haver de servir el seu cosí Euristeu, rei de Tirint, que li va imposar els treballs: matar el lleó de Nemea i la hidra de Lerna, capturar la cérvola de Cerinea i el senglar d'Erimant, netejar els estables d'Augias, espantar els ocells de l'Estímfal, portar el toro de Creta, les egües de Diomedes, el cinturó de l'amazona Hipòlita, els bous de Gerió, les pomes de les Hespèrides i, finalment, el gos Cèrber. Son of Zeus and the mortal Alcmene, Heracles was persecuted from the cradle by Hera, who sent two snakes that the baby strangled. As a man, Hera drove him mad and he killed his own children. To purify himself he had to serve his cousin Eurystheus, king of Tiryns, who set him the labours: kill the Nemean lion and the Lernaean hydra, capture the Ceryneian hind and the Erymanthian boar, clean the stables of Augeas, drive off the Stymphalian birds, bring the Cretan bull, the mares of Diomedes, the belt of the Amazon Hippolyta, the cattle of Geryon, the apples of the Hesperides and, last of all, the hound Cerberus. He died from the poison of the centaur Nessus, which his wife Deianira gave him thinking it a love potion. From the pyre he rose to Olympus and married Hebe.

Bellerophon, grandson of Sisyphus, tamed the winged horse Pegasus with a golden bridle Athena gave him. A guest of King Proetus of Tiryns, he rejected the queen, who falsely accused him. Proetus sent him to Lycia with a sealed tablet asking for his death: it is the only letter in Homer. The king of Lycia set him to kill the Chimera, a fire-breathing monster with a lion's head, a goat's body and a serpent's tail, and Bellerophon defeated it from the air. He passed other tests and married the king's daughter. But, in his pride, he tried to ride Pegasus up to Olympus; Zeus sent a gadfly, the horse threw him and Bellerophon wandered alone for the rest of his life.

Faetó, fill del Sol, va demanar al seu pare conduir un sol dia el seu carro de foc, com a prova que era el seu fill. Hèlios, que havia jurat concedir-li qualsevol desig, no s'hi va poder negar. Els cavalls es van desbocar: el carro va pujar massa i la terra es va gelar; va baixar massa i va cremar camps i ciutats. Zeus el va abatre amb el llamp, i Faetó va caure al riu Erídan. Les seves germanes, les Helíades, van plorar tant que es van convertir en pollancres, i les seves llàgrimes, en ambre.
Phaethon, son of the Sun, asked his father to let him drive his chariot of fire for one day, as proof that he was his son. Helios, who had sworn to grant him any wish, could not refuse. The horses bolted: the chariot climbed too high and the earth froze; it dropped too low and scorched fields and cities. Zeus struck him down with a thunderbolt, and Phaethon fell into the river Eridanus. His sisters, the Heliades, wept so much that they turned into poplars, and their tears into amber.

Níobe, reina de Tebes, tenia molts fills i filles i es va vanagloriar de ser més afortunada que la deessa Leto, que només en tenia dos. Aquells dos eren Apol·lo i Àrtemis, que van venjar la seva mare matant amb les seves fletxes tots els fills de Níobe. Ella, sense consol, es va convertir en una roca del mont Sípil, a Àsia, de la qual sempre brolla aigua, com si plorés. Niobe, queen of Thebes, had many sons and daughters and boasted of being luckier than the goddess Leto, who had only two. Those two were Apollo and Artemis, who avenged their mother by shooting all Niobe's children with their arrows. Inconsolable, she turned into a rock on Mount Sipylus, in Asia, from which water always flows, as if weeping. Homer already tells it in the Iliad, when Achilles invites Priam to eat in spite of his grief.

Adonis era un jove bellíssim, estimat per Afrodita. Va morir jove, ferit per un senglar durant una cacera, i la deessa el va plorar desconsolada. A Atenes, en ple estiu, les dones celebren les Adònies: sembren llavors en testos, els «jardins d'Adonis», que creixen de pressa i es marceixen de seguida, i pugen als terrats a plorar la seva mort amb cants. És una de les poques festes que les dones celebren a la seva manera, sense que l'organitzi la ciutat.
Adonis was a very beautiful youth, loved by Aphrodite. He died young, gored by a boar while hunting, and the goddess mourned him inconsolably. In Athens, in high summer, women celebrate the Adonia: they sow seeds in pots, the «gardens of Adonis», which grow quickly and wither at once, and climb onto the rooftops to lament his death with songs. It is one of the few festivals women celebrate in their own way, without the city organising it.
