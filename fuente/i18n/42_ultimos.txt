El gran port d'Atenes, a uns set quilòmetres de la ciutat i unit a ella pels Murs Llargs. Té tres ports naturals: el Càntar, per al comerç, i Zea i Muníquia, per als vaixells de guerra. Athens' great port, about seven kilometres from the city and joined to it by the Long Walls. It has three natural harbours: Kantharos, for trade, and Zea and Mounichia, for warships. Its streets were laid out in a grid by Hippodamus of Miletus. Here money is changed, Black Sea grain is bought and ships leave for the whole Mediterranean. In the game the port is much simplified: just a quay with a merchant ship.

Una pedra clavada en un camp: «Fita d'un terreny venut amb dret de recompra a Fanòstrat de Peània per mil dracmes». Quan una terra garantia un deute, el creditor hi posava un d'aquests horoi, perquè tothom sabés que no es podia vendre ni hipotecar de nou fins a liquidar el deute. Se n'han trobat centenars a l'Àtica. No hi havia cap registre públic de la propietat.
A stone set in a field: «Boundary of land sold with right of redemption to Phanostratus of Paeania for a thousand drachmas». When land secured a debt, the creditor set one of these horoi on it, so everyone would know it couldn't be sold or mortgaged again until the debt was paid. Hundreds have been found in Attica. There was no public land registry.

The ludus of the elementary teacher (ludi magister or litterator) is a pergola, a space open to the street, separated from the noise only by a curtain. Lessons begin before dawn, by lamplight, and go on until midday. Boys and some girls aged seven to eleven learn to read, to write on wax tablets and to count, escorted by their paidagogus. Teachers are known for their strictness: Horace remembered his teacher Orbilius as «the flogger». Talk to Flavius to attend his classes.
