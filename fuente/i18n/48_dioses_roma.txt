Salve. Sóc el pare Tiberí, el riu. Abans em deien Àlbula, per les meves aigües blanques; vaig canviar de nom quan s'hi va ofegar Tiberí, rei d'Alba. Vaig portar al meu corrent la cistella de Ròmul i Rem i la vaig deixar a la riba. Tot el que entra a Roma per aigua passa per mi.
Hail. I am Father Tiberinus, the river. Once they called me Albula, for my white waters; I changed my name when Tiberinus, king of Alba, drowned in me. I carried the basket of Romulus and Remus on my current and left it on the bank. Everything that enters Rome by water passes through me.

I was in flood that day and left them at the foot of the Palatine, beside the fig tree. Since then the Romans have prayed to me as a father. Horatius Cocles asked me to receive his arms before he leapt into the water, and I carried him to the bank. And young Cloelia, who escaped from Porsenna's camp with other hostages, I let her swim across.

Because many rivers and much rain feed me, and because men build ever closer to me. When I rise, I flood the Campus Martius, the Forum Boarium and the low-lying districts; the Romans go about their streets in boats. Tiberius appointed curators to watch over my banks, but I still do as I please.

Les Tiberinàlies, al desembre. I una vegada em vaig aparèixer en somnis a Enees, vell, coronat de canyes i amb un mantell verd, i li vaig dir que continués riu amunt, que trobaria una truja blanca amb trenta garrins: allà fundaria el seu fill Alba. Ho explica Virgili.
The Tiberinalia, in December. And once I appeared to Aeneas in a dream, old, crowned with reeds and wearing a green cloak, and told him to go on upriver, where he would find a white sow with thirty piglets: there his son would found Alba. Virgil tells it.
